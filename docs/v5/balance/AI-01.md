## Tuning done

- **swarm `lineWaitS` 3 → 10.** With the GDD's 3 the reference won only 29% (200 seeds). The cause is the swarm main line's loose `skirmish` shape on non-shield troops (celtic swordsmen): it wraps a line. Holding the line 10 s while the skirmishers work brings it to 43–44% and fits "skirmishes and feints". The steady and other doctrine values are the GDD's.
- **reserveWaver** is shifted by +0.15 for every doctrine. The GDD's table gives steady 0.5 "(today)", but today's ai.ts commits the reserve below 0.65 mean morale. steady keeps 0.65, so it stays today's AI.

## First contact

The GDD expected F1 alone to bring a 5v5 campaign opening from 33 s to under 15 s. Measured on campaign fields with a waiting player, F1 moves it only from 26.0 s to 25.2 s, because the press is not what holds the bot back:

1. Missiles reset the idle clock. The bot's skirmisher and the player's peltast trade shots, so the "8 s with no combat" press never fires.
2. A steady bot on high ground holds it for 50 s.
3. Even on a plain the march sets a floor. The line waits 5 s and then walks at about 0.9 paces a second, which gives contact at 18–22 s.

Bot against bot on an open field, a 5v5 meets in 11.9 s (AC 3 met). In the campaign the band kinds now set the pace: the playtest against a rush band (Galatae raiders) made contact at 13 s and was over at 0:40. An "opening press" (press at pressAfter even under missile fire, until first contact) was tried and dropped. It only reached 19 s, and it swung the ridge rows by 15 to 35 points.
