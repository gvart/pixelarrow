# FW-02 balance: works allowance, bot placement and the stand-off

`npm run balance` (200 seeds) ran on `v5` (9572f02) before the change and on the FW-02 branch after it. Targets come from design/gdd/field-works.md ("Balance targets") and ai-doctrines.md R5. A scratch harness also checked hash parity for battles without works.

## What ships

- **Allowance and supplies** (R4, `src/game/campaign.ts`):
  - `worksAllowance(supplies, caught)` counts, for each type, how many segments the supplies would buy on their own. Each count is capped at `WORKS.max` (3), or at `WORKS.caught` (1) when a band caught the player.
  - `canAffordWork(supplies, placed, type, caught)` is the tray's gate. The sim knows the allowance per type but not the purse.
  - `Campaign.wasCaught(party, byPlayer)` is true when the band was chasing, the player didn't attack, and he wasn't in camp.
  - `Campaign.worksLeft(placed)` gives the tray header.
  - `Campaign.payWorks(b.works())` charges only for the player's segments that are on the field at battle start, so an undone segment is never charged (R4.3). It never takes more than the supplies hold.
- **Garrison allowance** (R4.4, `src/game/enemy.ts`): `garrisonWorks()` gives 2 free stakes segments (`GARRISON_STAKES`). `EnemyArmy.works` is optional. Field bands get none, whatever their doctrine.
- **Bot placement** (R6, `src/sim/ai.ts` `placeWorks`): runs at the end of `BotAI.deploy`. With no allowance it issues nothing and draws nothing.
  - Garrison stakes: one segment per palisade gate gap, 2 paces out. If the deploy zone refuses that spot, it tries 1.5, 1 and then 0 paces.
  - Field stakes: placed across the corner of the main line nearest the enemy horse, which was FW-01's best measured spot. With no horse, they go 2 paces in front of the skirmishers.
  - Ditch: 2 paces ahead of the main line's front.
  - Laager: never.
  - Attackers path through works by terrain speed.
- **Stand-off** (ai-doctrines R5): applies to swarm and hammer. It starts when every enemy group still standing has held still (hold order, no contact, no move) for 20 s or more, at least one of them behind its side's works or in an orb, and our missile-men have ammo. While it lasts:
  - the press is suspended;
  - missile groups focus the nearest holder;
  - other groups not in contact hold.

  It ends for good when the ammo runs out or nobody has loosed for 10 s (`STAND_OFF_QUIET_S`). Then the bot presses.

## No-works parity (ladder, lairs, online)

A scratch harness hashed 279 battles before and after the change, and the results were **bit-identical**:

- all 50 ladder floors × 4 seeds, through `ladderSetup`, with both sides bot-driven;
- 20 matched battles;
- 10 ridge mirrors;
- 25 generated fields;
- hydra, cyclops, Nemean lion and minotaur × 6 seeds.

So all 50 floors stay inside V5-052's ±5 by construction, and the lair rows stay at V5-056. Tests prove:

- no ladder floor, duel, async or war setup (`onlineBattleSetup`) carries `works`;
- their bots issue no `work` order;
- a `work` order sent into one of them is refused (`allowance`).

## Decisions (made while the user was asleep)

1. **Garrison works aren't wired to a battle yet.** Lair garrisons and the palisade field are AR-02, which comes later and owns `world.ts` and `battlefield.ts`. FW-02 ships `garrisonWorks()`, `EnemyArmy.works` and the gate-gap placement, tested on a hand-built palisade grid. So no lair difficulty moves in FW-02, and AR-02 measures the 38–48% assault row.
2. **The player's allowance isn't passed into `campaignBattle.ts` yet.** That file isn't FW-02's. FW-03 (the tray) wires `Campaign.worksAllowance(wasCaught(...))` into `ArmySpec.works` and calls `payWorks(b.works())` at `startBattle`.
3. **Stand-off doctrines are a list in ai.ts** (`STAND_OFF_DOCTRINES`), not a doctrine field, because `src/data/doctrines.ts` isn't FW-02's. "Behind works" means the holders' side placed any segment.
4. **Stand-off exit.** It also ends after 10 s with no shot, so shooters left out of reach can't stall a battle into a draw. A first version without this gave 70% draws on the turtle rows.
5. **The ridge + works row uses the rush attacker** (`RIDGE_ATTACKER`), like the doctrine ridge row that carries the 62–68% target. A steady attacker gives the ridge 71% bare and 81% with works. Steady doesn't flank a hill, which is the outlier ai-doctrines.md fixes with `hillFlank`.
6. **More segments than R6 names.** Extra stakes go on the line's wings, horse side first. Extra ditches go 3 paces to either side.
7. **Side order.** A bot on side 0 deploys before side 1's groups exist, so it can't see the enemy horse and lays field stakes before its skirmishers. Campaign bots are always side 1, so this doesn't affect play.
8. **FW-01 rows clear bot-laid works** (`clearWorks`), and their no-works arms carry no allowance, so those rows still measure the works they name. The numbers came back identical.
